0Overview: one motor, two jobsThe motor lifts the car platform, then the same motion spins it for display.

Sequence

  1. Drive. The motor turns the toothed outer circle through a 7.25:1 gear pair.
  2. Lift. The outer circle's rotation pulls a string that raises the lifting arm 55 mm along a vertical slot.
  3. Grab. Two-stage grabbers tilt out and slide under the car platform as the arm rises.
  4. Release. At the top of the slot, the arm lifts a latch and locking pin, which frees the inner circle.
  5. Display. The inner circle now turns with the outer circle, rotating the car.
  6. Return. Reversing the motor lowers the arm, the pin drops back into the frame and locks the inner circle.

No second motor or sensor decides when to switch from lifting to rotating. The geometry does.

1Geared drive ringTurns the motor into slow, strong rotation of the outer circle.

How it works

  • A 40 mm gear on the NEMA 17 stepper meshes with the teeth on the 290 mm outer circle, a 7.25:1 reduction.
  • The outer circle turns 7.25 times slower than the motor, and in the ideal case delivers up to 7.25 times the torque.
  • It is the only driven part. It pulls the string that lifts the arm, and once the latch releases, it carries the inner circle around with it.
2String-and-spindle liftRaises the lifting arm 55 mm and multiplies the torque.

How it works

  • The large spindle is turned by the rotation of the large outer circle and pulls the string. This converts rotation of the circle into rotation about a perpendicular axis, which drives the lifting arm up.
  • The large and small spindles share one axle, so the large driven spindle multiplies the torque available at the string.
  • The arm travels 55 mm.

Design details

  • The string provides the pull, while the slope and vertical slot set the arm's path. Without the string, the arm would not rise straight.
  • The spool gives a smooth, consistent force that supports the lifting arm.
3Cam slope, vertical slot and guide railsKeep the arm moving only straight up and down.

How it works

  • The slope on the outer circle and the vertical slot together restrict both vertical and horizontal movement of the lifting arm.
  • The arm's extension runs in a specific slot that allows only vertical movement.

Design details

  • The arm clamps to the outer circle with ribs. Early on, the ribs sometimes fell into the slope gap as the circle rotated.
  • The guide rails fix this by restricting rotation of the arm, so it stays aligned with the inner circle.
4Two-stage grabbersSlide under the car platform and set it flush with the top surface.

How it works

  • The vertical slot restricts lateral motion. An angled edge at the bottom lets the grabbers tilt down along a centre pivot on the lifting arm.
  • Stage 1. The centre stage does the initial lifting. Its frame is longer and lower, so it meets the angled wall earlier and tilts out first.
  • Stage 2. The other two arms then come in and support the platform at a wider and higher level than the middle, flush with the top surface.

Design details

  • The finger-like ends slide under the platform without jamming.
5Latch and locking pinHold the display circle still during the lift, then release it at full height.

How it works

  • A locking pin sits in holes in the wood frame, held by a spring-loaded latch. This keeps the inner circle still while the outer circle turns.
  • At the top of the slot, the lifting arm lifts the latch, which lifts the pin out of its hole. The inner circle is now free to spin with the outer one.
  • When the motor reverses, the arm lowers, the latch drops and the pin falls into a hole, locking the inner circle again.

Why it matters

It sequences the two jobs mechanically, so no extra motor, sensor or code is needed to decide when to rotate.

6Clamp and steel-ball raceLet the inner circle turn freely but not move up or down.

How it works

  • The clamp has some play, so the inner circle rotates freely but cannot move vertically.
  • The bottom ring and the outer circle share a groove filled about 90% with 5 mm stainless-steel balls, which works like a ball bearing and makes the rotation smoother.
7Testing and known issueSmooth on the way down, a little jumpy on the way up.

Current status

The mechanism works. It moves smoothly on the way down and is slightly jumpy on the way up.

Possible causes to check

  • Friction or stick-slip where the arm's extension runs in the vertical slot.
  • Uneven string tension as it winds onto the spindle.
  • Motor acceleration that is too abrupt for the load, which can be softened in the motion settings.
